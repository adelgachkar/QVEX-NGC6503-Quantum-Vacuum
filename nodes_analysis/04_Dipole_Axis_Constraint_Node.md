## Node 4: Dipole-Axis Constraint and Coupling to the PID Controller

### 1. Physical Interpretation of the Hypothesis

The matter and gas of galaxy NGC 6503 are predominantly concentrated within a two-dimensional equatorial plane. The fundamental origin of this planar symmetry is the existence of an **energy dipole pair ($D_{\text{jet}}$)** generated by opposing relativistic jets emanating from the central supermassive black hole along the $\hat{z}$ axis:

$$\vec{D}_{\text{jet}} = P_{\text{jet}} \hat{z} - P_{\text{jet}} (-\hat{z})$$

This dipole pair establishes a lateral pressure gradient across the quantum vacuum foam. At polar angles $\theta \neq \pi/2$, the vacuum stress reaches its maximum, whereas along the equatorial plane ($\theta = \pi/2$), the potential gradient vanishes, creating a stable potential well:

$$V_{\text{eff}}(r, \theta) = -\frac{G M(r)}{r} + \frac{\alpha_{\text{dipole}} P_{\text{jet}}}{r^3} \cos^2(\theta)$$

> **Notational note:** The test implementation `tests/test_ngc6503_vacuum_nodes.py::dipole_potential` uses the reduced unit convention $-g\,m/r$ in place of $-GM(r)/r$; both are the same Newtonian term under $GM(r) = g\,m$.
